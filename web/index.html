Re-detect black hole mergers from raw detector data

Pick an event on the left. The app downloads the raw strain recorded by LIGO Hanford, LIGO Livingston and Virgo, estimates each detector's noise, and correlates the data with 500+ waveform templates for merging black holes. A detection needs a coincident signal in at least two detectors within the light-travel time between them, a signal shape that matches a merger (χ² test), and a network SNR louder than every one of 400 time-shifted background trials.

To look for new signals, open the Survey tab: it scans long stretches of public data, measures a false-alarm rate, flags anything not in the catalogues, and every candidate gets a Contribute panel that writes the report, drafts the email and publishes to Zenodo with a DOI.

You then get the whitened signal with the best-fit waveform on top, time-frequency maps where the "chirp" is visible, the estimated chirp mass compared with the published value, and the signal converted to audio.

Data: Gravitational Wave Open Science Center (gwosc.org).
